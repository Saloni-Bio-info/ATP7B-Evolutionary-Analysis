# Comparative Evolutionary Analysis of ATP7B Across Vertebrates

## 1. Project Objective

This project investigates the evolutionary conservation and selection patterns of ATP7B across vertebrates using comparative sequence analysis, multiple sequence alignment, phylogenetic reconstruction, protein conservation analysis and codon-based selection tests.

ATP7B encodes a copper-transporting P-type ATPase involved in maintaining copper homeostasis. Pathogenic variants in human ATP7B are associated with Wilson disease.

The primary objectives were to:
- Identify and retrieve ATP7B orthologues across vertebrates.
- Assess sequence conservation and evolutionary relationships.
- Examine conservation across the six annotated heavy metal-associated (HMA) domains.
- Investigate gene-wide and site-specific selection patterns.
- Identify and interpret candidate sites under positive selection.

## 2. Dataset

ATP7B orthology information was retrieved from Ensembl.

- 174 one-to-one orthology relationships were identified.
- 82 protein sequences and corresponding coding DNA sequences (CDS) were successfully retrieved.
- Human ATP7B (UniProt: P35670; 1,465 amino acids) was included as a reference.
- A practical protein-length threshold of 1,300 amino acids was applied to select the primary dataset.
- Final protein dataset: 72 sequences (71 orthologues + human).
- Final codon dataset: 71 orthologues (human excluded because the corresponding human CDS was unavailable).
- Protein alignment length: 2,076 amino-acid positions.
- Codon alignment length: 2,076 codons (6,228 nucleotides).

The length threshold was used as a practical filtering criterion and does not represent a biological definition of ATP7B orthology.

## 4. Analysis Workflow

### A. Orthologue Retrieval and Quality Control

ATP7B orthologues were identified using Ensembl one-to-one orthology information. Protein and CDS sequence pairs were retrieved and checked for translation consistency and sequence quality.

A total of 82 protein/CDS pairs passed the initial retrieval and quality assessment. A practical protein-length threshold of 1,300 amino acids was subsequently applied to construct the primary dataset.

### B. Protein Multiple Sequence Alignment

The 72 ATP7B protein sequences were aligned using MAFFT.

Input:
`results/ATP7B_72_filtered_aligned.fasta`

Original alignment statistics:
- Sequences: 72
- Alignment columns: 2,076
- Mean gap percentage: 30.48%
- Columns with at least 50% gaps: 601
- Columns with at least 70% gaps: 559
- Columns with at least 90% gaps: 491

### C. Core Alignment Sensitivity Analysis

A core alignment was generated by removing terminal overhang regions while retaining all 1,465 amino-acid residues of the human ATP7B reference sequence.

Core alignment:
`ATP7B_72_core_aligned.fasta`

Comparison of original and core alignments:

| Metric | Original | Core |
|---|---:|---:|
| Alignment columns | 2,076 | 1,770 |
| Mean gap percentage | 30.48% | 19.93% |
| Columns with ≥50% gaps | 601 | 305 |
| Columns with ≥70% gaps | 559 | 287 |
| Columns with ≥90% gaps | 491 | 246 |

The mean gap percentage decreased by approximately 34.6% following terminal trimming.

Conservation statistics remained unchanged:
- Evaluated positions: 1,423
- Mean conservation: 77.28%
- Highly conserved positions (≥90% identity): 638

These results indicate that terminal trimming reduced alignment gaps without changing the evaluated conservation statistics.

### D. Phylogenetic Reconstruction

Maximum-likelihood phylogenetic reconstruction was performed using IQ-TREE.

Original alignment:
- Best-fitting model according to BIC: Q.MAMMAL+I+R6
- 1,000 ultrafast bootstrap replicates
- 1,000 SH-aLRT replicates

Core alignment:
- Best-fitting model according to BIC: Q.MAMMAL+I+R5
- 1,000 ultrafast bootstrap replicates
- 1,000 SH-aLRT replicates

The original and core phylogenies contained the same 72 taxa. Their normalised Robinson–Foulds distance was 0, indicating identical unrooted tree topology after terminal trimming.

The reconstructed phylogeny represents the ATP7B gene tree and should not automatically be interpreted as a species tree.

Tree files are available in `trees/`.

### E. Protein Conservation Analysis

Conservation was calculated across the 72-sequence protein alignment.

Final statistics:
- Total alignment positions: 2,076
- Evaluated positions: 1,423
- Mean conservation: 77.28%
- Highly conserved positions (≥90% identity): 638

The same conservation results were obtained using the core alignment under the applied coverage criterion.

### F. HMA Domain Conservation

Conservation was evaluated across six annotated HMA domains using canonical human ATP7B coordinates from UniProtKB entry P35670.

| Domain | Human coordinates | Mean conservation |

| HMA2 | 143–209 | 75.98% |
| HMA3 | 257–323 | 73.25% |
| HMA4 | 359–425 | 73.10% |
| HMA5 | 488–554 | 80.87% |
| HMA6 | 564–630 | 82.81% |

HMA6 exhibited the highest mean conservation, whereas HMA1 exhibited the lowest.

### G. Codon Alignment and Quality Assessment

The codon alignment was generated by back-translating the protein alignment using the corresponding CDS sequences.

Input:
`results/ATP7B_71_codon_aligned.fasta`

Validation:
- 71 sequences
- 2,076 codons
- 6,228 nucleotide positions
- No incomplete codon gaps
- No premature stop codons

The codon alignment was used for downstream selection analyses.

### H. Gene-wide Selection Analysis Using BUSTED

BUSTED (Branch-Site Unrestricted Statistical Test for Episodic Diversification) was applied to investigate evidence of episodic diversifying selection across the ATP7B gene.

Results:
- Likelihood-ratio statistic: 430.51
- Reported p-value: 0, reflecting numerical reporting precision.

Estimated rate categories:

| Selection category | Estimated dN/dS | Estimated proportion |
|---|---:|---:|
| Negative selection | 0.061 | 78.985% |
| Negative selection | 0.667 | 20.716% |
| Diversifying selection | 184.057 | 0.299% |

The results provide evidence consistent with episodic diversifying selection at the gene level. The estimated proportions indicate that most sites belong to negatively selected rate categories.

BUSTED is a gene-wide test and does not independently identify the specific site responsible for the selection signal.

### I. Site-specific Selection Analysis Using FEL

Fixed Effects Likelihood (FEL) analysis was performed across 2,076 codon alignment positions.

Results:
- Total sites tested: 2,076
- Sites classified under purifying selection: 1,190
- Sites classified as not significant: 875
- Candidate positive-selection sites at uncorrected p ≤ 0.1: 11

Following Benjamini–Hochberg correction at FDR 0.05:
- One site remained statistically significant.
- Significant alignment position: 850
- Uncorrected p-value: 0.009239
- Adjusted p-value: 0.020063

The 11 candidate positive-selection alignment positions were:

151, 195, 204, 615, 792, 806, 809, 840, 850, 1585 and 1937.

FEL evaluates pervasive site-level selection across the tested phylogeny. Its results should be interpreted alongside the gene-wide BUSTED analysis.

### J. Candidate Site Mapping

Alignment position 850 was mapped to human ATP7B residue S478 using the human reference sequence.

Site statistics:
- Alignment position: 850
- Human residue: S478
- Amino acid: Serine
- Alpha: 0.50382
- Beta: 1.58983
- Uncorrected p-value: 0.009239
- Adjusted p-value: 0.020063

The site is located between the annotated HMA4 domain (359–425) and HMA5 domain (488–554), outside the six annotated HMA domains.

Serine was observed in 26 of 72 protein sequences (36.1%) at the corresponding alignment column.

Five of the 11 FEL candidate sites mapped to positions in the human reference sequence, while six corresponded to gaps in the human sequence.

### K. Structural Investigation of S478

The experimental human ATP7B structure PDB 7XUM was examined. Its resolved chain A covered residues 562–1405 and therefore did not include S478.

A full-length AlphaFold model of human ATP7B (AF-P35670-F1, version 6) was subsequently retrieved.

S478 was present in the model, but its predicted local confidence was very low:

- Residues 450–477: mean pLDDT 31.91
- S478: pLDDT 27.33
- Residues 479–510: mean pLDDT 65.23

Consequently, the predicted three-dimensional environment of S478 was not considered sufficiently reliable for detailed structural or mechanistic interpretation.

### L. Functional Annotation of S478

External functional annotation resources report phosphorylation annotation at S478, with PRKD1 among the associated kinase annotations. S481 is also reported as a phosphorylation site.

The overlap between the FEL selection signal and the reported phosphorylation annotation provides a potential direction for further investigation.

However, this observation does not establish a causal relationship between phosphorylation and positive selection, nor does it demonstrate a functional or disease-related effect of S478.

## 5. Main Findings and Interpretation

The comparative evolutionary analysis indicates substantial conservation of ATP7B across the sampled vertebrate sequences.

The principal findings are:

1. ATP7B exhibits substantial sequence conservation, with 638 highly conserved positions identified.
2. The six HMA domains show different levels of conservation, with HMA6 being the most conserved.
3. Terminal trimming substantially reduced alignment gaps without changing the evaluated conservation statistics or the unrooted phylogenetic topology.
4. BUSTED provided evidence consistent with episodic diversifying selection at the gene level.
5. FEL identified 11 candidate positive-selection sites, of which only alignment position 850 remained significant after multiple-testing correction.
6. Alignment position 850 corresponds to human ATP7B residue S478.
7. S478 has a reported phosphorylation annotation, but its structural interpretation remains limited by very low AlphaFold confidence.

Overall, the results indicate that purifying selection predominates across ATP7B, with evidence of gene-wide episodic diversifying selection and one statistically significant FEL candidate site.

## 6. Limitations

1. The primary protein dataset was filtered using a practical sequence-length threshold of 1,300 amino acids. This may introduce taxonomic or sequence-annotation bias.
2. The codon analysis included 71 non-human orthologues because the corresponding human CDS was unavailable.
3. Codon alignment was generated by back-translation from a protein alignment.
4. The initial 11 FEL candidate sites were identified using uncorrected p-values of ≤0.1; only one remained significant after multiple-testing correction.
5. BUSTED provides gene-wide evidence and does not independently establish which individual site drives the signal.
6. The AlphaFold model showed very low local confidence at S478, preventing reliable structural interpretation.
7. The phosphorylation annotation is a database-derived observation and does not establish a causal connection with evolutionary selection.
8. The analysis depends on sequence quality, alignment accuracy, taxon sampling and phylogenetic reconstruction.
9. The workflow is not yet a completely automated end-to-end pipeline. Some analyses and plotting steps were performed through terminal commands or interactive Python scripts.
